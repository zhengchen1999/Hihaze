Abstract

Diffusion models have shown promising performance in real-world image dehazing. However, restoring high-resolution images remains challenging due to spatially non-uniform haze and inconsistent predictions across local patches. To address these issues, we propose Hihaze, a one-step diffusion framework for high-resolution real-world dehazing. Hihaze adopts two haze-aware modulation modules: attentive skip compensation (ASC) and a spatial degradation adapter (SDA). ASC selectively transfers reliable encoder information to the variational autoencoder (VAE) decoder, preserving structural details while suppressing haze-degraded features. SDA extracts multi-scale spatial degradation representations and injects them into the U-Net to guide restoration according to local haze variations. Furthermore, we design a training-free global-guided patch fusion (GPF) method. It uses a global prediction to align patch appearance and downweight unreliable predictions, followed by structure-guided detail compensation. Notably, this training-free design also makes GPF a plug-and-play module for other dehazing models. Evaluations on four real-world benchmarks demonstrate that Hihaze outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both restoration quality and inference efficiency. Hihaze achieves PSNR gains of 3.6 dB on Dense-Haze and 3.6 dB on NH-HAZE over the best competing methods. Code will be made publicly available.

Method

SDA guides spatially adaptive denoising, while ASC transfers reliable encoder features to the VAE decoder. GPF combines global and local predictions for high-resolution restoration with consistent appearance and preserved fine details.
